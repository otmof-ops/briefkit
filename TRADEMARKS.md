The short version: use the name to say what your thing is made of or works
with; do not use it to say what your thing *is*.

## Free to do, no permission needed

- Say that a document was **generated with BriefKit**, or that a tool **works
  with BriefKit**, **extends BriefKit** or **is based on BriefKit**, when that
  is accurate.
- Write templates, variants, color presets, editor plugins, CI integrations and
  teaching material for it. The configuration keys, the template names and the
  command's flags are not claimed as trademarks or as protected expression.
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons, reviews and
  criticism, including unflattering ones.
- Distribute unmodified official releases under the BriefKit name, with
  `LICENSE` and `NOTICE.txt` intact, as the license requires.
- Name a package, plugin or repository in a way that describes its relationship
  truthfully, such as `briefkit-templates-acme`, `briefkit-action` or
  `awesome-briefkit`, provided it is clear the project is not the official one.

## Ask first

- Naming a **fork, product, service, company or domain** "BriefKit" or a
  confusingly similar name (Briefkit, BriefKits, BreifKit, briefkit.dev). Forks
  are welcome under BSD-3-Clause; they need their own name once they differ
  from the official releases.
- Using the **logo** anywhere other than to link to the official project.
- Calling a build, distribution or service **official**, **certified**, or
  otherwise implying endorsement.
- Merchandise carrying the name or logo.

Open an issue at https://github.com/otmof-ops/briefkit/issues with what you
have in mind, or write to the address in `SECURITY.md`. The usual answer to a
truthful, non-confusing use is yes.

## House style, which keeps the mark a mark

- BriefKit is one word, capital B, capital K. Use it as an adjective for a
  thing ("the BriefKit CLI", "a BriefKit template"), not as a verb or a generic
  noun for PDF generators.
- The command and the package are `briefkit`; the configuration file is
  `briefkit.yml`. Neither is claimed as a mark.
- The first prominent use in a document may carry ™ (or ® once a registration
  exists; none is claimed as registered today). After that, plain text.

## Why this exists

BSD-3-Clause lets anyone copy, change and sell the code. It does not let anyone
sell it as BriefKit, or pass their own work off as this project, and its
copyright-notice condition keeps the author's credit on every copy. This policy
is the part of that arrangement the license leaves to the trademark owner. It
is written to be easy to comply with, and it is not a license to the software
itself: that is `LICENSE`.
